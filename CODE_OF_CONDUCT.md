# Code of Conduct

## Purpose

Freeloader is a small technical project. This document exists so that everyone who takes part knows what is expected of them and what to do when something goes wrong.

## Expected behaviour

- Keep criticism on the work: the code, the design, the measurement. Not on the person who submitted it.
- Assume the other side is competent and acting in good faith until there is evidence otherwise.
- Be precise. "This deadlocks when the queue limit is lowered while a transfer runs" helps; "this is broken" does not.
- Accept that a well-argued no is a legitimate outcome. Scope decisions belong to the maintainers.
- Respect the time of others. Read the existing issues, the requirements and the design notes before opening a duplicate.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, intimidation or sustained hostility, in issues, pull requests, commit messages or private contact.
- Insults or demeaning remarks about a person or a group, including remarks about origin, gender, sexual orientation, disability, appearance, age, religion or level of experience.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention of any kind.
- Publishing private information about another person without their consent.
- Deliberately derailing discussions, or repeatedly reopening a settled decision without new arguments.
- Contributing code, tests or documentation whose purpose is to create a false impression of correctness, security or completeness.

## Scope

This applies in every project space: the repository, issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages and any communication that identifies itself as being about Freeloader.

## Reporting

Report a concern privately to the maintainers by opening a [GitHub security advisory](https://github.com/HuberLeon007/freeloader/security/advisories/new), which is a private channel, or by contacting the repository owner ([@HuberLeon007](https://github.com/HuberLeon007)) directly.

Include what happened, where, when, and links if they exist. Reports are handled confidentially. The reporter is not asked to justify their discomfort.

## Enforcement

The maintainers respond in proportion to what happened:

1. A private clarification of which behaviour was a problem and why.
2. A public correction in the affected thread.
3. A temporary block from participation.
4. A permanent block.

Deliberate misrepresentation of a report is itself a violation. Decisions rest with the maintainers, who will explain them to the people involved.
